# FAA Part 107 Study Cards

Cards are original learning prompts mapped to FAA-S-ACS-10B. Expand the answer
only after attempting it. `Critical` marks a rule or decision whose omission
could make an operation illegal or unsafe.

## I. Regulations

### REG-01 · UA.I.A.K1 · Critical

**Q:** What aircraft weight falls within the Part 107 small-UAS rule?

**A:** Less than 55 pounds at takeoff, including everything onboard or attached.
An aircraft at exactly 55 pounds is not “less than 55.” Other applicability
conditions still must be checked.

### REG-02 · UA.I.A.K2

**Q:** What is the difference between the unmanned aircraft and the sUAS?

**A:** The unmanned aircraft is the flying device; the system includes the
aircraft and associated elements needed for safe and efficient operation, such
as the control station and command link.

### REG-03 · UA.I.A.K4 · Critical

**Q:** When and where is a qualifying Part 107 accident reported?

**A:** Report to the FAA within 10 calendar days when the operation causes
serious injury, loss of consciousness, or at least $500 damage to property other
than the sUAS. Use the FAA's current reporting channel and preserve the facts.

### REG-04 · UA.I.A.K5

**Q:** What must a remote PIC provide to the FAA on request?

**A:** Required records/documents and the small UAS for inspection or testing;
the pilot may also have to demonstrate regulatory compliance.

### REG-05 · UA.I.B.K1 · Critical

**Q:** Does a 0.50-pound aircraft used under Part 107 require registration?

**A:** Yes. Each aircraft operated under Part 107 is individually registered.
The at-or-below-0.55-pound exception is limited to qualifying recreational use.

### REG-06 · UA.I.B.K2 · Critical

**Q:** Who decides whether the complete sUAS is in safe condition before flight?

**A:** The remote PIC. Manufacturer guidance and crew inspections inform the
decision but do not transfer responsibility.

### REG-07 · UA.I.B.K4 · Critical

**Q:** May a non-certificated person manipulate the controls?

**A:** Yes, only under the direct supervision of a certificated remote PIC who
can immediately take direct control. The remote PIC retains authority and responsibility.

### REG-08 · UA.I.B.K5

**Q:** May the remote PIC deviate from Part 107 during an emergency?

**A:** To the extent necessary to respond to an in-flight emergency. The FAA may
request a written report of the deviation; the emergency is not blanket relief.

### REG-09 · UA.I.B.K6 · Critical

**Q:** When may an object be dropped from an sUAS?

**A:** Only when doing so does not create an undue hazard to people or property.
Other payload, hazardous-material, and carriage rules still apply.

### REG-10 · UA.I.B.K7

**Q:** What is the moving-vehicle rule most applicants should remember?

**A:** Never operate from a moving aircraft. Operation from a moving land/water
vehicle is allowed only over a sparsely populated area and not when transporting
another person's property for compensation or hire, unless authorized otherwise.

### REG-11 · UA.I.B.K8 · Critical

**Q:** State the three core alcohol restrictions.

**A:** No operation within 8 hours after drinking, with blood alcohol at or above
0.04%, or while under the influence/effects that impair safety. Drug/medication
impairment is also prohibited.

### REG-12 · UA.I.B.K9 · Critical

**Q:** What is required for routine Part 107 night flight?

**A:** Updated initial/recurrent night knowledge and anti-collision lighting
visible for at least 3 statute miles with a flash rate sufficient to avoid a
collision. All airspace and other rules remain applicable.

### REG-13 · UA.I.B.K10 · Critical

**Q:** What must VLOS allow the crew to determine?

**A:** The aircraft's location, attitude, altitude, direction of flight, and
whether it endangers life or property, with unaided vision except corrective lenses.

### REG-14 · UA.I.B.K11

**Q:** What makes a visual observer arrangement valid?

**A:** Effective communication with the remote PIC/person manipulating controls
and coordinated awareness sufficient to maintain required VLOS and hazard avoidance.

### REG-15 · UA.I.B.K12

**Q:** May one person be remote PIC or visual observer for two simultaneous flights?

**A:** No. A person may not act as remote PIC or VO for more than one operation at once.

### REG-16 · UA.I.B.K14 · Critical

**Q:** Who has right-of-way: the sUAS or any manned aircraft?

**A:** The manned aircraft. The sUAS must yield and remain well clear; never pass
over, under, or ahead unless well clear.

### REG-17 · UA.I.B.K16 · Critical

**Q:** Which airspace generally requires prior ATC authorization under §107.41?

**A:** Class B, C, D, and the lateral boundaries of surface-area Class E
designated for an airport.

### REG-18 · UA.I.B.K20

**Q:** What does the preflight assessment cover beyond inspecting the aircraft?

**A:** Local weather, airspace/restrictions, people/property, surface hazards,
flight/landing areas, and other risks; brief operating and emergency procedures.

### REG-19 · UA.I.B.K21 · Critical

**Q:** State the baseline speed, altitude, visibility, and cloud-clearance limits.

**A:** 87 knots/100 mph groundspeed; normally 400 ft AGL; 3 statute miles flight
visibility; 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontally from clouds.

### REG-20 · UA.I.B.K21b

**Q:** What is the structure exception to 400 ft AGL?

**A:** The aircraft may fly higher when within 400 ft of a structure, but no more
than 400 ft above the structure's immediate uppermost limit. Airspace authorization
and all other restrictions still apply.

### REG-21 · UA.I.B.K26

**Q:** Name core conditions for carrying property for compensation or hire.

**A:** Total takeoff weight remains under 55 lb, VLOS and other Part 107 rules are
met, the operation occurs within a state (with specified DC/Hawaii treatment),
and the flight is not conducted from a moving vehicle/aircraft under the prohibited condition.

### REG-22 · UA.I.C.K3

**Q:** What are the Remote Pilot Certificate eligibility requirements?

**A:** At least 16, English capable, physically/mentally safe, and either pass
UAG or qualify through the Part 61/current-flight-review training path, plus TSA vetting.

### REG-23 · UA.I.C.K4

**Q:** How often must aeronautical knowledge recency be renewed?

**A:** Complete qualifying recurrent training within the preceding 24 calendar
months to exercise Part 107 privileges.

### REG-24 · UA.I.D.K1

**Q:** How does a waiver differ from an airspace authorization?

**A:** A waiver grants relief from a specified waivable operating rule after an
equivalent-safety showing. An authorization permits specified controlled-airspace
access. One does not automatically provide the other.

### REG-25 · UA.I.E.K3 · Critical

**Q:** What makes an aircraft eligible for Category 1 operations over people?

**A:** It weighs 0.55 lb or less throughout the operation, including attachments,
and has no exposed rotating parts capable of causing lacerations. Sustained flight
over open-air assemblies additionally requires Remote ID compliance.

### REG-26 · UA.I.E.K3

**Q:** What distinguishes Category 2 and Category 3 aircraft eligibility?

**A:** Both use FAA-accepted performance-based means/declarations of compliance,
labeling, and injury/rotating-part requirements. Category 3 permits a higher
injury threshold but imposes stricter operating limits and no open-air assemblies.

### REG-27 · UA.I.E.K3

**Q:** What defines Category 4 eligibility?

**A:** An eligible aircraft with a Part 21 airworthiness certificate, operated
and maintained within its approved flight manual/FAA limitations.

### REG-28 · UA.I.E.K1 · Critical

**Q:** Does a brief direct crossing above a person's arm count as “over” a person?

**A:** Yes. “Over” includes flight directly above any part of a person regardless
of duration. “Sustained flight” is a separate concept for assemblies/vehicles.

### REG-29 · UA.I.F.K1

**Q:** What does Standard Remote ID broadcast that a module does not?

**A:** Standard RID includes the moving control-station location/elevation and
emergency status; a module reports takeoff location/elevation instead.

### REG-30 · UA.I.F.K2 · Critical

**Q:** What additional operational limit applies to a Remote ID broadcast module?

**A:** The pilot must maintain VLOS. The module serial must be correctly associated
with the Part 107 aircraft's registration record.

### REG-31 · UA.I.F.K3

**Q:** When can a non-Remote-ID aircraft operate in a FRIA?

**A:** Only within the FRIA's geographic boundaries and VLOS, while satisfying
all other applicable rules. A FRIA is not general permission for Part 107 activity.

## II. Airspace

### AIR-01 · UA.II.A.K1

**Q:** On a sectional, what commonly depicts Class B, C, D, and surface Class E?

**A:** Class B solid blue, Class C solid magenta, Class D dashed blue, and
airport surface Class E dashed magenta. Always confirm with the current legend.

### AIR-02 · UA.II.A.K1

**Q:** A Class C shelf is labeled `40/SFC`. What does it mean?

**A:** The segment extends from the surface to 4,000 ft MSL; sectional figures
drop the final two zeros. Determine the actual segment and terrain before converting.

### AIR-03 · UA.II.A.K1

**Q:** Why is confusing MSL and AGL dangerous?

**A:** Airspace/chart elevations often use MSL while Part 107 height limits use
AGL. Failing to add terrain elevation can enter controlled airspace or misjudge clearance.

### AIR-04 · UA.II.A.K2

**Q:** Contrast prohibited and restricted areas.

**A:** Prohibited areas bar flight for security/welfare reasons without permission;
restricted areas contain unusual/hazardous activity and require checking status/authorization.

### AIR-05 · UA.II.A.K2

**Q:** Do MOAs automatically prohibit sUAS flight?

**A:** Not generally, but they signal military activity and elevated collision
risk. Check status, controlling agency information, authorization needs, and safety.

### AIR-06 · UA.II.A.K3 · Critical

**Q:** Why must TFRs be checked near launch time?

**A:** They can arise or change after planning and impose temporary legal
restrictions for VIP, emergency, wildfire, stadium, security, or other activity.

### AIR-07 · UA.II.B.K2

**Q:** Is a UAS Facility Map grid value an authorization?

**A:** No. It is a planning value used in authorization processing. The pilot
must receive and comply with an actual LAANC or FAA authorization.

### AIR-08 · UA.II.B.K2

**Q:** What does LAANC further coordination do?

**A:** It routes a request above a facility-map value, including a zero grid, for
ATC review while remaining at or below 400 ft. FAA guidance calls for at least
72 hours before the requested start.

### AIR-09 · UA.II.B.K3 · Critical

**Q:** Does airspace authorization near an airport guarantee separation from traffic?

**A:** No. The remote PIC must still see, avoid, yield, monitor likely traffic,
and comply with authorization conditions and all operating rules.

### AIR-10 · UA.II.B.K4

**Q:** Name four hard-to-see low-altitude hazards.

**A:** Wires, unlit towers/structures, balloons/kites, and aircraft such as
helicopters or agricultural aircraft; thermal plumes and laser activity are additional hazards.

### AIR-11 · UA.II.B.K9

**Q:** What do red, green, and white aircraft position lights help infer?

**A:** Orientation/direction: red is left wing, green right wing, white tail.
Interpretation is a collision-avoidance clue, never a substitute for yielding.

## III. Weather

### WX-01 · UA.III.A.K2

**Q:** Is a METAR an observation or forecast, and what time standard does it use?

**A:** An observation, normally timestamped in UTC (`Z`).

### WX-02 · UA.III.A.K3

**Q:** Is a TAF an observation or forecast?

**A:** A terminal-area forecast over a stated validity period; apply the forecast
group covering the mission time.

### WX-03 · UA.III.A.K2

**Q:** Decode `22015G25KT 3SM`.

**A:** Wind from 220° true at 15 knots gusting 25, visibility 3 statute miles.
The gust and aircraft/site limits may make the operation unsafe even at legal visibility.

### WX-04 · UA.III.A.K2

**Q:** Which sky-cover groups constitute a reported ceiling?

**A:** The lowest broken (`BKN`) or overcast (`OVC`) layer, or vertical visibility
into an obscuration (`VV`); few/scattered layers are not ceilings.

### WX-05 · UA.III.A.K2

**Q:** Decode `BKN008 18/17 A2980`.

**A:** Broken ceiling at 800 ft AGL, temperature 18°C, dew point 17°C, altimeter
29.80 inHg. The close spread suggests high relative humidity/fog potential.

### WX-06 · UA.III.B.K1a

**Q:** What conditions increase density altitude?

**A:** Higher temperature, higher field elevation, and lower pressure. Humidity
has a smaller additional effect. Higher density altitude reduces aerodynamic/thrust margin.

### WX-07 · UA.III.B.K1b · Critical

**Q:** Why can launching downwind create a battery trap?

**A:** The return becomes a headwind leg with higher power and time demand when
the battery is already depleted. Plan reserve for the worst leg.

### WX-08 · UA.III.B.K1c

**Q:** Contrast stable and unstable air operationally.

**A:** Stable air tends toward smooth conditions, haze/fog/stratus; unstable air
supports turbulence, gusts, vertical development, and showery weather.

### WX-09 · UA.III.B.K1e · Critical

**Q:** What is the correct small-UAS strategy for thunderstorms and microbursts?

**A:** Avoid them with generous margins. Rapid outflow, lightning, hail,
turbulence, and severe downdrafts exceed safe small-UAS capability.

### WX-10 · UA.III.B.K1g

**Q:** Why is airframe icing hazardous?

**A:** It adds weight and drag, distorts lift/thrust surfaces, reduces efficiency,
and can cause rapid loss of controllability; avoid known/suspected icing conditions.

## IV. Loading and performance

### PERF-01 · UA.IV.A.K1

**Q:** What must be included in takeoff weight?

**A:** Aircraft, installed battery, payload, guards, lights, mounts, cargo, and
everything onboard or attached.

### PERF-02 · UA.IV.A.K1b

**Q:** How can an out-of-limit center of gravity affect flight?

**A:** Reduced stability/control authority, unequal motor load, increased energy
use, and unexpected automated behavior or inability to recover.

### PERF-03 · UA.IV.A.K1a

**Q:** What is the typical chain from added payload to reduced mission margin?

**A:** More lift/thrust required → higher current/heat → reduced acceleration,
climb, and endurance → less wind and emergency reserve.

### PERF-04 · UA.IV.A.K2

**Q:** Why use manufacturer performance data instead of a previous flight alone?

**A:** Performance changes with configuration, temperature, altitude, wind,
battery health, and loading. One anecdotal flight does not establish safe limits.

### PERF-05 · UA.IV.A.K1

**Q:** A 52-lb aircraft receives a 4-lb payload. Can it remain under Part 107?

**A:** No. Takeoff weight becomes 56 lb, which is not less than 55 lb.

## V. Operations

### OPS-01 · UA.V.A.K2

**Q:** What is CTAF used for?

**A:** Monitoring/announcing traffic information at an airport without an
operating tower. Monitoring improves awareness but silence does not prove no traffic.

### OPS-02 · UA.V.A.K4

**Q:** What is UNICOM?

**A:** A nongovernment air-ground communication facility that may provide airport
advisory information; it is not an ATC clearance service.

### OPS-03 · UA.V.A.K5

**Q:** What does ATIS provide?

**A:** A continuous recorded broadcast of current terminal information such as
weather, approaches/runways, and notices, identified by a phonetic letter.

### OPS-04 · UA.V.A.K7

**Q:** Spell `UAS` using the aviation phonetic alphabet.

**A:** Uniform Alpha Sierra.

### OPS-05 · UA.V.B.K3

**Q:** What does runway 27 indicate?

**A:** An approximate magnetic heading of 270°. Parallel runways add L, C, or R.

### OPS-06 · UA.V.B.K4 · Critical

**Q:** Where should a remote pilot expect concentrated manned-aircraft traffic?

**A:** Runway approach/departure paths, traffic-pattern legs and turns, helicopter
routes/pads, and low-level special operations—though traffic can occur anywhere.

### OPS-07 · UA.V.B.K6

**Q:** What two FAA sources help identify airport frequencies and details?

**A:** Current aeronautical charts and the Chart Supplement.

### OPS-08 · UA.V.B.K7

**Q:** What should occur after a wildlife strike?

**A:** Land safely, assess injury/damage, preserve details, and use the applicable
FAA wildlife/accident reporting processes. Reinspect before return to service.

### OPS-09 · UA.V.C.K1

**Q:** What makes an emergency plan usable?

**A:** Site-specific triggers, immediate actions, crew words/roles, safe landing
areas, contact methods, configured aircraft behavior, and practiced decision points.

### OPS-10 · UA.V.C.K2 · Critical

**Q:** Name warning signs that a lithium battery should be removed from service.

**A:** Swelling, puncture, crushed case, damaged leads, leakage/odor, abnormal heat,
water/contamination exposure, or abnormal voltage/performance.

### OPS-11 · UA.V.C.K3

**Q:** Why must return-to-home settings be site-specific?

**A:** An automatic climb/straight return can hit structures, enter airspace,
overfly people, fight wind, or exhaust the battery. Verify altitude, route, and behavior.

### OPS-12 · UA.V.C.K4

**Q:** What behavior may follow GNSS loss?

**A:** Loss of position hold, drift with wind, unreliable automated routes/RTH,
and increased manual-control workload; land safely using the briefed response.

### OPS-13 · UA.V.D.K1

**Q:** What does PAVE stand for?

**A:** Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures—a preflight risk scan.

### OPS-14 · UA.V.D.K2

**Q:** What is the purpose of crew resource management?

**A:** Use people, information, equipment, communication, and workload effectively
to identify errors/hazards and make safer decisions.

### OPS-15 · UA.V.D.K3

**Q:** What are signs of lost situational awareness?

**A:** Fixation, missed callouts, uncertainty about aircraft position/energy,
falling behind events, contradictory mental models, and unrecognized condition changes.

### OPS-16 · UA.V.D.K4

**Q:** Name the five hazardous attitudes.

**A:** Anti-authority, impulsivity, invulnerability, macho, and resignation.
Use the corresponding antidotes: follow rules; think first; it can happen to me;
taking chances is foolish; I am not helpless.

### OPS-17 · UA.V.D.K5

**Q:** Give the basic risk-management loop.

**A:** Identify hazard → assess likelihood/severity → choose controls → reassess
residual risk → accept, modify, delay, relocate, or cancel → monitor continuously.

### OPS-18 · UA.V.E.K1

**Q:** Name early heat-illness/dehydration effects relevant to a remote pilot.

**A:** Headache, thirst, fatigue, dizziness, impaired concentration/judgment, and
slower response. Stop, cool, hydrate, and obtain medical help for serious symptoms.

### OPS-19 · UA.V.E.K3 · Critical

**Q:** Is an over-the-counter medication automatically safe for flight?

**A:** No. Label warnings and individual side effects such as drowsiness matter;
when effects or fitness are uncertain, do not operate and seek appropriate guidance.

### OPS-20 · UA.V.E.K5

**Q:** What does IMSAFE stand for?

**A:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion/Eating.

### OPS-21 · UA.V.E.K8

**Q:** How should a pilot preserve dark adaptation?

**A:** Avoid unnecessary bright/white light, dim displays, use appropriate low-
intensity lighting, allow adaptation time, and scan off-center for faint objects.

### OPS-22 · UA.V.E.K9

**Q:** What is autokinesis?

**A:** A stationary light can appear to move when stared at against a dark,
featureless background. Use references, scanning, and instrument/crew cross-checks.

### OPS-23 · UA.V.F.K1

**Q:** Who may maintain a typical Part 107 sUAS without a Category 4 requirement?

**A:** Part 107 generally does not require an FAA mechanic certificate, but the
remote PIC must ensure safe condition and follow applicable manufacturer,
category, declaration, and operational requirements.

### OPS-24 · UA.V.F.K2 · Critical

**Q:** What systems belong in a complete preflight inspection?

**A:** Structure, fasteners/arms/gear, propulsion/props, battery/retention,
payload, controller/display/antennas, command link, software/configuration,
GNSS/compass, Remote ID, required lighting, and an operational control check.

### OPS-25 · UA.V.F.K3

**Q:** What should happen after a firmware or configuration change?

**A:** Record it, confirm compatibility/settings, inspect/calibrate only as
manufacturer directs, and conduct a controlled functional test before a demanding mission.

### OPS-26 · UA.V.F.K4

**Q:** Why keep maintenance and battery records if Part 107 does not prescribe a universal log format?

**A:** They establish configuration, airworthiness decisions, recurring defects,
battery degradation, category/waiver compliance, and defensible corrective action.
