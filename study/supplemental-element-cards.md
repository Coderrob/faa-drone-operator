# Supplemental ACS Element Cards

These original cards fill element-level gaps in the core deck. Each answer is a
study summary; follow the source link/locator in the canonical inventory and
[regulatory index](../docs/09-regulatory-index.md) for controlling detail.

| Card ID / ACS | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| SUP-UA-I-A-K3 / UA.I.A.K3 | What is the consequence of falsifying an application, certificate, authorization, record or report? | It can support denial, suspension or revocation and enforcement; never alter or knowingly make a fraudulent statement in required aviation records. |
| SUP-UA-I-A-K6 / UA.I.A.K6 | Can one aircraft qualify in more than one operations-over-people category? | It may meet multiple category bases, but the remote PIC must identify and comply with the category used for that operation and its records, configuration and restrictions. |
| SUP-UA-I-A-K7 / UA.I.A.K7 | What determines record retention for an operation? | The specific rule, category, waiver or authorization controls; retain required declarations/instructions/maintenance and approval evidence for its stated period and availability requirement. |
| SUP-UA-I-A-K8 / UA.I.A.K8 | Does being manufactured before the OOP/RID rules automatically exempt an aircraft? | No. Previously manufactured status may affect available compliance paths, but the current operation must meet Part 89/107 or a valid authorization/exception. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K3 / UA.I.B.K3 | When must a person decline a crew role for medical reasons? | Whenever a physical or mental condition would interfere with safe operation; fitness is reassessed throughout the mission. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K6a / UA.I.B.K6a | What is the practical test for careless or reckless operation? | Do not operate in a manner that endangers another person's life or property; formal limits do not excuse an operation that is foreseeably unsafe. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K13 / UA.I.B.K13 | May a Part 107 aircraft carry hazardous material? | No under §107.36; determine classification from applicable hazardous-material rules rather than assuming a consumer product is harmless. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K17 / UA.I.B.K17 | What duty applies near an airport even with authorization? | Do not interfere with airport operations or traffic patterns; monitor, yield, remain well clear and relocate/land when needed. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K22 / UA.I.B.K22 | Who may manipulate Part 107 flight controls? | A certificated/current remote pilot, or a person under direct supervision of one who can immediately take direct control. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K23 / UA.I.B.K23 | Do automated routes transfer remote-PIC responsibility to software? | No. The remote PIC must monitor the system, maintain all operating requirements and be able to intervene or terminate safely. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K24 / UA.I.B.K24 | How is civil twilight treated for lighting? | Apply the current §107.29 definition/conditions and qualifying anti-collision lighting; calculate local official sunrise/sunset rather than estimating ambient light. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K27 / UA.I.B.K27 | May an sUAS use an ATC transponder? | Not unless authorized by the FAA under the applicable rule; ordinary Part 107 operation does not install/use one merely to be visible. |
| SUP-UA-I-B-K28 / UA.I.B.K28 | May an sUAS transmit ADS-B Out? | Generally prohibited unless authorized; ADS-B Out also cannot substitute for Remote ID. |
| SUP-UA-I-C-K1 / UA.I.C.K1 | Why do drug/alcohol offenses matter after certification? | Specified offenses and required reports can affect certificate eligibility or enforcement; comply with current reporting and operating prohibitions. |
| SUP-UA-I-C-K2 / UA.I.C.K2 | What can refusal to provide required drug/alcohol test results cause? | Refusal or failure to furnish results can support denial, suspension or revocation under Part 107 certification rules. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K2 / UA.I.E.K2 | What extra gates apply when flying over people at night? | Both the applicable OOP category and night requirements must pass, plus RID for sustained flight over open-air assemblies where required. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K4 / UA.I.E.K4 | How should the operating area be selected for OOP? | Consider trajectory and foreseeable failure area, keep nonparticipants protected, and use access control/containment appropriate to the category. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K5 / UA.I.E.K5 | Is there one universal horizontal distance from every person? | No. Apply the selected category and operating conditions; avoid direct overflight unless expressly eligible and use risk-based standoff for failure trajectory. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K6 / UA.I.E.K6 | When may Category 1–3 aircraft operate over moving vehicles? | Within/over a closed or restricted-access site with occupants on notice, or without sustained flight over moving vehicles, while meeting the category; Category 4 follows approved limits. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K7 / UA.I.E.K7 | What must happen after modifying an OOP-eligible aircraft? | Determine whether the change affects eligibility, declaration, required/optional components, instructions or maintenance; do not assume eligibility survives. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K8 / UA.I.E.K8 | What makes a site closed or restricted access for Category 3/vehicle rules? | Access is actually controlled and affected people receive required notice; a public place does not become restricted by merely posting a small sign. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K9 / UA.I.E.K9 | What should the remote PIC do with OOP operating instructions? | Possess, understand and follow the current instructions for configuration, limitations, inspections, maintenance and component replacement. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K10 / UA.I.E.K10 | Why track required components and category declaration? | Category eligibility depends on the declared configuration; missing/substituted required components can invalidate the basis for flight over people. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K11 / UA.I.E.K11 | Can an optional component be ignored in OOP planning? | No. Even if optional for eligibility, its installed weight, failure mode and effect on configuration/performance must be evaluated. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K12 / UA.I.E.K12 | Can an applicant-built or modified aircraft self-declare Category 2/3 eligibility casually? | No. It must follow the applicable FAA-accepted means/declaration process and meet production, labeling, instruction and records requirements. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K13 / UA.I.E.K13 | Who submits a Declaration of Compliance and what does a pilot do with it? | The responsible producer submits it; the pilot verifies FAA acceptance and the exact aircraft/configuration rather than submitting a consumer declaration. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K14 / UA.I.E.K14 | What maintenance principle protects OOP eligibility? | Follow declared instructions, use permitted parts/processes, document work and confirm the aircraft continues to meet its category basis before release. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K15 / UA.I.E.K15 | What is a Means of Compliance? | An FAA-accepted method showing how a design meets performance requirements; it is distinct from the producer's Declaration of Compliance. |
| SUP-UA-I-E-K16 / UA.I.E.K16 | Why does impact kinetic energy matter? | Category 2/3 performance limits address injury severity from impact; actual aircraft weight, speed and design/configuration affect that energy. |
| SUP-UA-I-F-K3 / UA.I.F.K3 | Is aeronautical research a general RID exception? | No. §89.120 requires specific FAA authorization for operations solely for research or showing regulatory compliance. |
| SUP-UA-I-F-K4 / UA.I.F.K4 | Can ADS-B Out satisfy Remote ID? | No; §89.125 expressly says it cannot, and separate ADS-B Out prohibitions may apply. |
| SUP-UA-I-F-K5 / UA.I.F.K5 | What is Confirmation of Identification used for? | It documents a foreign-registered civil unmanned aircraft's Part 89 notice; it stays at the control station, must be current and is not U.S. registration. |
| SUP-UA-I-F-K7 / UA.I.F.K7 | What should a pilot confirm about RID product labeling? | The device/aircraft labeling and serial correspond to an FAA-accepted declaration and the serial recorded in registration; labeling alone is not proof of function. |
| SUP-UA-II-A-K4 / UA.II.A.K4 | What is ATC's role versus the remote PIC's role? | ATC manages authorized airspace access/traffic services; the remote PIC retains responsibility for authorization compliance, VLOS, yielding and safety. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4a / UA.II.B.K4a | Name common causal themes to counter in flight planning. | Poor decisions, inadequate preflight, loss of awareness/control, weather, fuel/energy mismanagement and failure to see/avoid; use layered controls. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4b / UA.II.B.K4b | Why avoid flight beneath unmanned balloons? | Suspended payloads, lines and unpredictable drift create collision/entanglement hazards that may be hard to see. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4c / UA.II.B.K4c | Should a drone approach another aircraft to inspect an emergency? | Not without appropriate authority/coordination; remain well clear and never add a collision hazard to an airborne emergency. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4d / UA.II.B.K4d | What can precipitation static do? | Charge buildup can interfere with radio/navigation equipment and signals; avoid unsuitable precipitation and respond to degraded links. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4e / UA.II.B.K4e | What should occur after laser illumination of an aircraft? | Protect vision/control, terminate or land safely, preserve time/location details and use current FAA/law-enforcement reporting guidance. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K4f / UA.II.B.K4f | Why avoid smoke-stack and cooling-tower plumes? | Strong vertical flow, turbulence, heat, moisture/contaminants and poor visibility can overwhelm a small aircraft. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K6 / UA.II.B.K6 | What operator equipment supports night flight? | Compliant aircraft lighting plus suitable display/flashlight, visibility aids, spare power and site controls without destroying night vision. |
| SUP-UA-II-B-K7 / UA.II.B.K7 | Why learn obstruction lighting? | It helps locate towers/structures and interpret hazards, but failed/obscured lights mean visual confirmation and conservative clearance remain necessary. |
| SUP-UA-III-A-K1 / UA.III.A.K1 | What makes a complete aviation weather briefing? | Current observations/forecasts, winds, ceilings/visibility, hazards/advisories and trends from authoritative sources matched to route, altitude and time. |
| SUP-UA-III-A-K4 / UA.III.A.K4 | What do weather charts add beyond one METAR? | Spatial pattern and movement of fronts, pressure, precipitation/clouds and hazards; use issuance/valid times and legends. |
| SUP-UA-III-A-K5 / UA.III.A.K5 | What is the ASOS/AWOS limitation for a drone site? | It is a point observation at sensor height/location; terrain, obstacles and distance can make actual low-level site wind/visibility different. |
| SUP-UA-III-B-K1d / UA.III.B.K1d | What changes commonly accompany a front? | Wind shift/gusts, pressure and temperature change, cloud/precipitation and possible turbulence/thunderstorms; time the mission against passage. |
| SUP-UA-III-B-K1f / UA.III.B.K1f | What is the correct response to tornado potential? | Do not launch; seek official warnings and personal shelter. Protecting equipment is secondary to life safety. |
| SUP-UA-III-B-K1h / UA.III.B.K1h | Why avoid hail even if stones are small? | It can damage props/sensors/structure, accompany severe convection and create loss of control. |
| SUP-UA-III-B-K1i / UA.III.B.K1i | How does fog affect legality and safety? | It reduces flight visibility/VLOS, may create a low ceiling/obscuration and deposits moisture; wait for verified improvement. |
| SUP-UA-III-B-K1k / UA.III.B.K1k | Why avoid lightning environments? | Electrical discharge, associated convection/gusts/precipitation and electromagnetic effects create unacceptable risk. |
| SUP-UA-V-A-K1 / UA.V.A.K1 | How do towered and non-towered airport awareness differ? | Towered traffic may receive ATC service; non-towered pilots coordinate by advisory calls. In both cases the drone must not interfere and must yield. |
| SUP-UA-V-A-K3 / UA.V.A.K3 | What belongs in a useful manned-aircraft self-announcement? | Airport, aircraft identification/type, position/altitude and intention, repeated airport name; remote pilots mainly monitor unless authorized/competent to transmit. |
| SUP-UA-V-A-K6 / UA.V.A.K6 | Why recognize call signs and registration numbers? | They identify aircraft/agency and support interpreting traffic calls; never confuse a call sign with a runway or frequency. |
| SUP-UA-V-A-K8 / UA.V.A.K8 | How are altitude, direction, speed and time communicated clearly? | Use standard aviation phraseology/units, cardinal or clock references as briefed, and UTC/local distinction; avoid ambiguous casual wording. |
| SUP-UA-V-B-K1 / UA.V.B.K1 | Name airport types that can produce low-level traffic. | Towered/non-towered airports, airports with part-time towers, heliports and seaplane bases; each may have traffic beyond obvious runways. |
| SUP-UA-V-B-K2 / UA.V.B.K2 | Why monitor tower communications near an airport? | Calls reveal runway use, arrivals/departures and conflicts, improving awareness; monitoring does not equal an authorization or clearance. |
| SUP-UA-V-B-K5 / UA.V.B.K5 | What is a SIDA? | A security-controlled airport area with identification/access rules; airspace authorization does not grant ground access. |
| SUP-UA-V-B-K8 / UA.V.B.K8 | What airport lights should a remote pilot recognize? | Runway/taxiway/approach/beacon and seaplane-base cues sufficiently to locate operating surfaces and traffic, using current airport references. |
| SUP-UA-V-C-K5 / UA.V.C.K5 | Why can frequency-spectrum limitations cause emergencies? | Interference, obstruction, antenna orientation or congestion can degrade command/video/GNSS; plan lost-link behavior and do not trust advertised range. |
| SUP-UA-V-C-K6 / UA.V.C.K6 | What is the immediate priority in an OOP abnormal event? | Protect exposed people: maneuver/land/terminate toward the safest area, communicate, secure the site and preserve/report evidence as applicable. |
| SUP-UA-V-C-K7 / UA.V.C.K7 | What changes in a night emergency? | Reduced visual cues and unlit obstacles constrain options; use surveyed alternates, lighting and crew callouts, avoiding a blind automated route. |
| SUP-UA-V-D-K1a / UA.V.D.K1a | What makes team communication effective? | Standard callouts, closed-loop acknowledgement, shared plan, explicit roles/handoffs and authority for anyone to call an abort. |
| SUP-UA-V-D-K1b / UA.V.D.K1b | How should task saturation be managed? | Aviate/maintain control first, shed nonessential mission tasks, delegate, slow/hold/land and avoid fixation. |
| SUP-UA-V-E-K2 / UA.V.E.K2 | Can a pilot operate after eight hours if still impaired? | No. Eight hours is only one restriction; any alcohol/drug impairment remains disqualifying, as do applicable BAC and medication effects. |
| SUP-UA-V-E-K4 / UA.V.E.K4 | How should hyperventilation be handled? | Recognize rapid breathing/dizziness/tingling, reduce workload, consciously slow breathing and land; seek medical help when symptoms persist or diagnosis is uncertain. |
| SUP-UA-V-F-K6 / UA.V.F.K6 | What is added to a night preflight? | Verify anti-collision light function/visibility/secure mounting, aircraft orientation cues, display/flashlights, unlit obstacles and daylight-survey assumptions. |
| SUP-UA-V-F-K7 / UA.V.F.K7 | Why verify a Category 2/3 manufacturer DoC before flight? | It is part of the aircraft's eligibility basis; confirm FAA acceptance, exact model/configuration, label, instructions and continued maintenance conformity. |
