# Registration and Application Processes

“Registering” can mean four different tasks. Complete the ones that apply:

1. IACRA applicant registration and FTN.
2. PSI knowledge-test registration.
3. IACRA Remote Pilot Certificate application.
4. FAA DroneZone aircraft registration and Remote ID inventory.

## A. Create the IACRA applicant account and obtain an FTN

Before beginning, have a stable email address, legal name and suffix, residential
address, mailing address if different, date of birth, and identification handy.

1. Open [IACRA](https://iacra.faa.gov/IACRA/Default.aspx) and select **Register**.
2. Select the **Applicant** role. Other roles do not replace Applicant.
3. Accept the Terms of Service and continue.
4. Complete the user profile. If no FAA certificate is held, leave the existing-
   certificate section blank.
5. Enter the full legal name exactly as it appears on identification. It must
   match the knowledge-test record. Include a suffix when applicable.
6. Enter a working email address and create the username/password.
7. Resolve any validation messages and submit registration.
8. Record the displayed FTN; IACRA also emails it. Keep it permanently—do not
   create another IACRA identity for a later application.

The FTN is safe to enter into the FAA testing workflow but should still be
treated as personal account information. Do not put credentials, birth dates,
or identification images in this repository.

## B. Register for the UAG knowledge test

1. Go to the [PSI FAA portal](https://faa.psiexams.com/faa/login).
2. Create/sign in to the testing account using identity information that matches
   IACRA and supply the FTN.
3. Select **Unmanned Aircraft General – Small (UAG)**. Verify that it is the
   initial certification test, not recurrent training.
4. Choose the test center, appointment, and payment method. Confirm the current
   fee, rescheduling/cancellation terms, and accommodation procedure in PSI.
5. Review the FAA/PSI identification matrix. Bring the accepted original,
   unexpired identification and any approved accommodation documents.
6. After testing, retain the AKTR. Verify the name, FTN/test data, score, and
   17-digit Knowledge Test Exam ID.

Test facts from the ACS: 60 questions, two hours, 70% pass. A passing result is
usable for the application for 24 calendar months. A failed applicant waits 14
calendar days before retesting.

## C. Apply for the Remote Pilot Certificate after passing UAG

1. Wait up to 48 hours for the result to reach IACRA.
2. Sign in to IACRA and choose **Start New Application**.
3. Select application type **Pilot**.
4. Select certification **Remote Pilot**.
5. Complete **Other Path Information** and start the application.
6. Complete every Form 8710-13 section. Check legal name, addresses, citizenship,
   physical-description fields, certificate history, and answers before signing.
7. Enter the 17-digit Knowledge Test Exam ID when prompted.
8. Review, electronically sign, and submit.
9. Monitor email and IACRA. After TSA security vetting, follow the instructions
   to retrieve/print the temporary certificate.
10. Verify the permanent card when it arrives. Correct discrepancies through
    the FAA process rather than creating another profile.

Carry the certificate and government-issued photo identification while acting
as remote PIC, ready to present to authorized persons under the rule.

## D. Part 61 training-course application

Only use this path when holding a Part 61 pilot certificate and a flight review
within the preceding 24 calendar months.

1. Complete FAASTeam course **ALC-451** and retain its completion certificate.
2. In IACRA start `Pilot → Remote Pilot`, choosing the training-course basis.
3. Finish and sign the application.
4. Arrange validation with an FSDO, DPE, ACR, or CFI.
5. Bring IACRA application ID/FTN, photo identification, Part 61 certificate,
   flight-review evidence, and ALC-451 certificate.
6. Follow the validator/IACRA instructions for temporary and permanent issuance.

## E. Register each Part 107 aircraft in FAA DroneZone

Source: [FAA aircraft registration instructions](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone)

For Part 107, every aircraft used must be individually registered, including an
aircraft at or below 0.55 lb. The recreational-only exception for aircraft at or
below 0.55 lb does not apply to Part 107. A recreational registration cannot be
transferred into a Part 107 registration.

Prepare:

- Owner's physical and mailing addresses, email, and phone.
- Aircraft manufacturer/make and model.
- Standard Remote ID or broadcast-module type and its exact Remote ID serial
  number, when applicable.
- Payment card.
- Confirmation that the aircraft or module appears on an FAA-accepted Remote ID
  Declaration of Compliance when that is the compliance basis.

Workflow:

1. Open [FAA DroneZone](https://faadronezone-access.faa.gov/) and create/sign in
   to the owner account.
2. Launch **Drone Owners and Pilots Dashboard**.
3. Select the **Part 107 Dashboard**, not Recreational Flyer.
4. Open **Manage Device Inventory** and choose **Add Device**.
5. Answer the Remote ID question accurately.
6. Select **Standard Remote ID drone** or **Remote ID broadcast module**, as
   applicable. For a module, enter the aircraft make/model and the module's RID
   serial number as directed.
7. Enter the Remote ID serial exactly; distinguish it from the aircraft serial,
   Wi-Fi identifier, registration number, or controller serial.
8. Add the device, review the record, complete payment, and finish registration.
9. Download/save the registration certificate. Part 107 registration currently
   costs $5 **per aircraft** and is valid for three years.
10. Mark the aircraft with the FAA registration number on an external surface
    so it is accessible and legible under the marking rule.
11. Carry paper or digital proof during flight. A person operating the owner's
    aircraft must also have the certificate available.
12. Set a renewal reminder and update/cancel inventory records when facts change.

For an already-listed Part 107 aircraft gaining Remote ID, use the device's
Actions menu, select **Edit**, change the Remote ID answer to **Yes**, enter the
RID serial, and save, following the current DroneZone prompts.

### Registration decision table

| Operation | Aircraft weight | Registration treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| Part 107 | 0.55 lb or less | Register each aircraft |
| Part 107 | Over 0.55 lb and under 55 lb | Register each aircraft in DroneZone |
| Recreational exception only | 0.55 lb or less | FAA registration exception may apply |
| Any operation | 55 lb or more | Part 48 small-drone online path does not apply; use applicable FAA aircraft-registration/certification path |

## F. Validate Remote ID before flight

Registration does not prove the equipment is broadcasting correctly.

1. Confirm one legal method: Standard Remote ID aircraft, broadcast module, or
   operation inside a FRIA under all FRIA conditions. Limited FAA authorizations
   are case-specific, not a general fourth consumer option.
2. Check the [FAA Declaration of Compliance system](https://uasdoc.faa.gov/) for
   the aircraft/module when applicable. Manufacturers—not pilots—submit DoCs.
3. Confirm the DroneZone inventory contains the correct RID serial.
4. Power up and complete the Remote ID preflight status check. Do not launch if
   the aircraft indicates the Remote ID equipment is not functioning unless the
   operation is legally permitted by another method.
5. With a broadcast module, maintain VLOS and understand that it broadcasts the
   takeoff location rather than a moving control-station location.

## Frequent registration failures

- Creating PSI or DroneZone records before obtaining/matching the FTN.
- Using a nickname, omitted suffix, or changed name in only one system.
- Confusing the FTN, exam ID, airman certificate number, aircraft registration
  number, and Remote ID serial number.
- Selecting the Recreational dashboard for an aircraft intended for Part 107.
- Entering an aircraft/controller serial where the RID serial is requested.
- Assuming sub-250 g aircraft need no registration under Part 107.
- Marking the aircraft but failing to carry the registration certificate.
- Treating registration, Remote ID compliance, or a LAANC grid as permission to fly.
