# Progressive Flight Training Syllabus

Part 107 has no FAA practical test, but knowledge-test passage does not establish
flight competence. Conduct training in a legal, controlled area with an
appropriately qualified remote PIC. Manufacturer limits and site risk controls
override these generic tolerances.

## Rating scale

| Rating | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Not attempted or intervention required |
| 1 | Completes with coaching; unsafe/unstable error occurs |
| 2 | Completes safely with minor correction; explains decisions |
| 3 | Consistently precise, anticipates risk, and handles variation unaided |

Progress requires every critical item at 2 or higher on two separate flights.
Mission release requires the final scenario at 2+ with no instructor takeover.

## Lesson 1 — System setup and ground safety

Objectives: identify components/limitations; inspect structure, propulsion,
battery, controller, link, RID and configuration; establish launch zone; brief
roles; arm/disarm safely.

Standard: checklist completed without omitted critical item; correct aircraft,
home point, lost-link action and flight limits verbally confirmed.

## Lesson 2 — Basic control and orientation

Maneuvers: takeoff; stationary hover; yaw 90/180/360 degrees; forward/side/rear
translation; square pattern; stabilized approach and landing.

Baseline standard in light wind: remain within approximately 10 feet laterally
and 10 feet vertically of assigned hover, land within a 10-foot target, maintain
orientation, and never create an incursion. Tighten for aircraft/mission needs.

## Lesson 3 — Reversed orientation and manual recovery

Maneuvers: nose-in hover, figure eight, unexpected orientation callout, controlled
flight with reduced positioning assistance when the manufacturer permits.

Standard: selects correct control response without prolonged trial-and-error;
recovers/lands when automation or positioning is degraded. Never disable safety
features where manufacturer/site conditions make that unsafe.

## Lesson 4 — VLOS, scanning and crew coordination

Tasks: establish practical VLOS boundary; use VO bearing/traffic calls; positive
control handoff; screen-to-aircraft scan cycle; manned-aircraft injection.

Standard: remote PIC identifies position/attitude/direction, responds immediately
to `TRAFFIC`, yields/lands, and uses unambiguous handoff words. Loss of sight
triggers the briefed recovery rather than continued mission pressure.

## Lesson 5 — Energy, wind and contingencies

Tasks: outbound/upwind planning; battery decision points; alternate landing;
simulated high wind, low battery, payload shift warning, and landing-zone incursion.

Standard: begins return/landing before reserve threshold, selects safe alternate,
and prioritizes people/aircraft over equipment or mission completion.

## Lesson 6 — Abnormal and emergency procedures

Tabletop first, then safely simulate where supported: command-link degradation,
GNSS loss, flyaway indication, propulsion warning, RID failure, battery/fire after
landing, injury and unexpected aircraft.

Standard: states memory priority, executes aircraft-specific action, communicates,
lands/terminates safely, preserves evidence, and identifies reporting analysis.
Do not intentionally create an actual flyaway, battery fire or unsafe system failure.

## Lesson 7 — Night proficiency

Prerequisites: daytime site survey, remote-pilot night knowledge current, compliant
lighting, airspace/legal gates complete.

Tasks: lighting inspection; dark adaptation; display management; identification
of aircraft orientation lights; hover/pattern/landing; autokinesis and false-
horizon discussion; unlit-obstacle scenario.

Standard: maintains conservative boundary and stable control, correctly explains
night illusions, and aborts when glare or unlit hazards defeat reliable separation.

## Lesson 8 — End-to-end mission check

Candidate receives a realistic mission containing airspace, weather, people,
client pressure and one injected abnormal condition. Candidate must prepare the
mission record, obtain/interpret needed documents, brief crew, inspect, fly,
respond, log and debrief.

### Evaluation rubric

| Domain | Critical pass evidence |
|---|---|
| Legality | Correct pilot, aircraft, RID, airspace, OOP/night/waiver determination |
| Planning | Current sources, defensible limits, hazards and alternates documented |
| Aircraft | Complete inspection/configuration and serviceability decision |
| Control | Stable orientation, VLOS and landing without intervention |
| Traffic | Immediate yield and well-clear response |
| Energy | Timely return/land decision with usable reserve |
| Crew | Roles, handoffs and emergency words understood |
| ADM | Rejects unsafe external pressure and reassesses changes |
| Abnormal | Correct priority/action/communication and safe termination |
| Records | Accurate flight, defect, maintenance and incident analysis |

## Signoff record

```text
Pilot / instructor or evaluator:
Aircraft / configuration / location / date:
Lesson and scenario:
Conditions and automation available:
Critical-item ratings and evidence:
Instructor interventions:
Remediation assigned:
Result: repeat / supervised only / released for defined mission envelope
Pilot and evaluator signatures:
```

Release is limited to the documented aircraft, mission type and operating
envelope. Re-evaluate after long inactivity, material aircraft/configuration
change, incident, repeated unstable performance, or expansion into night,
complex airspace, OOP, mapping, inspection or other specialized work.
