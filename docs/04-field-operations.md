# Field Operations Checklists

These are adaptable baseline checklists. Add manufacturer-specific items and
mission-specific controls. A checklist supports—not replaces—remote PIC judgment.

## Mission planning (before travel)

- [ ] Name the remote PIC; confirm certificate and knowledge recency.
- [ ] Define objective, location, time window, route/area, maximum altitude,
  payload, crew, and client/site constraints.
- [ ] Confirm aircraft registration, marking, Remote ID method, and category
  eligibility for any operation over people.
- [ ] Determine airspace from current aeronautical data; obtain required FAA
  authorization or waiver and read every condition.
- [ ] Check TFRs, NOTAMs, airport/heliport activity, special-use airspace, and
  nearby emergency/security activity.
- [ ] Check forecast and expected wind aloft, gusts, visibility, ceiling,
  precipitation, temperature, density-altitude effects, and solar/night timing.
- [ ] Identify people, roads/moving vehicles, buildings, wires/towers, RF
  interference, wildlife, launch/recovery areas, and alternate landing sites.
- [ ] Define VLOS plan, observer positions, lost-link/RTH altitude and route,
  battery minimums, geofence behavior, and abort criteria.
- [ ] Obtain site/landowner permission and address local restrictions, privacy,
  insurance, and client requirements.
- [ ] Pack aircraft, controller, charged/logged batteries, spares, props/tools,
  lighting, landing marker, fire response equipment, first aid, and documents.

## On-site go/no-go

- [ ] Recheck airspace status, authorization, TFRs/NOTAMs, weather, and site changes.
- [ ] Remote PIC is fit under IMSAFE; no crew impairment or unsafe distraction.
- [ ] Establish takeoff/landing zone and keep nonparticipants clear.
- [ ] Brief roles, communications, aircraft callout, sterile periods, manned-
  aircraft response, lost link, injury/fire response, and abort authority.
- [ ] Inspect frame, fasteners, arms, landing gear, motors, props, guards,
  payload, battery condition/retention, controller, antennas, screen, and cables.
- [ ] Verify correct aircraft/profile, firmware/configuration status, storage,
  home point, GNSS/compass health, control link, battery values, Remote ID, and
  anti-collision light when required.
- [ ] Confirm manufacturer and mission limits; perform control/hover check where safe.
- [ ] Final scan: people, traffic, aircraft, obstacles, wind, and escape path.

## During flight

- [ ] Maintain VLOS and scan for manned aircraft; yield immediately and remain well clear.
- [ ] Stay inside altitude, speed, airspace, authorization, weather, and site limits.
- [ ] Monitor link, GNSS, battery trend, propulsion warnings, payload, wind drift,
  crew workload, people/vehicles, and landing-zone integrity.
- [ ] Make deliberate battery decisions using distance, wind, temperature, and
  alternate landing needs—not the displayed percentage alone.
- [ ] Abort, land, or relocate when a limit is approached, assumptions fail, the
  crew loses situational awareness, or safety is uncertain.

## Emergency priorities

1. Protect people and other aircraft.
2. Maintain or regain control if practical; execute the briefed contingency.
3. Communicate clearly to crew and affected persons/agencies.
4. Land or terminate the flight in the safest available area.
5. Address injury/fire, secure the site, preserve logs/evidence, notify required
   parties, and determine whether FAA accident reporting is required.

Do not chase an uncontrolled aircraft in a way that creates a second hazard.
For a battery fire or injury, call emergency services when appropriate and tell
responders that lithium batteries are involved.

## Postflight

- [ ] Power down safely; inspect for heat, impact, cracks, prop damage, swelling,
  contamination, or loose components.
- [ ] Quarantine and label damaged batteries/equipment; do not recharge until safe.
- [ ] Record date, location, aircraft, remote PIC/crew, flight time, battery,
  maintenance status, authorization/waiver, anomalies, incidents, and action taken.
- [ ] Back up required logs/media securely and follow privacy/data-retention commitments.
- [ ] Debrief what changed, what nearly failed, and what must be corrected before reuse.
